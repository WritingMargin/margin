This is the lunch to make when you want something bright, crunchy, and generous without turning on the stove. Chickpeas do the substantial part; the dressing brings everything together.

**Serves 2 · Ready in about 15 minutes · No cooking**

## Gather your ingredients

- 1 × 400 g tin chickpeas, drained and rinsed (about 240 g drained)
- ½ cucumber, diced
- 200 g cherry tomatoes, halved
- ½ red pepper, diced
- ¼ small red onion, finely chopped
- 2 large handfuls of salad leaves, washed and dried
- 1 small handful of parsley, roughly chopped
- 50 g feta, optional

**For the lemon dressing:** 2 tablespoons olive oil, 1 tablespoon lemon juice, 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard, a small pinch of salt, and black pepper.

[Checklist title="Before you start"]
- [ ] Drain and rinse the chickpeas
- [ ] Wash and chop the vegetables
- [ ] Find a clean jar for the dressing
[/Checklist]

## Shake the dressing

Put the dressing ingredients in a clean jar, close the lid, and shake until combined. Taste on a piece of cucumber. If it is sharper than you like, add another teaspoon of oil; if it tastes flat, try a few more drops of lemon.

## Chop, toss, and finish

Combine the chickpeas, cucumber, tomatoes, pepper, onion, and parsley in a large bowl. Pour over the dressing and toss well.

Divide the salad leaves between two bowls. Spoon the chickpea mixture over them and crumble over the optional feta. Serve with bread if you want a bigger lunch.

## Choose a variation

[Tabs title="Choose your bowl" variant="boxed" accent="#42543b"]
[Tab title="Creamy"]
Add half an avocado, sliced, instead of the feta. A spoonful of plain yogurt on the side is another option.
[/Tab]
[Tab title="Crunchy"]
Finish with 2 tablespoons of toasted pumpkin seeds. Scatter them on after dressing the salad so they keep their texture.
[/Tab]
[Tab title="Pantry swap"]
Use drained white beans instead of chickpeas, chopped spring onion instead of red onion, and a little red-wine vinegar in place of lemon juice.
[/Tab]
[/Tabs]

## A small skill that helps

A little finely chopped onion goes a long way. Watch the [one-minute onion lesson](/post/watch-and-cook), then take your time with the knife. The goal is even pieces, not speed.
